Plan: a user-to-user marketplace, financial advisors first

Build a Facebook Marketplace–style app for one vertical, financial advisors in the Philippines. Advisors post listings, the listings form a location-based feed, and clients either message a listing or opt in to be contacted. Phone numbers are only shared once both sides agree on a meeting time, and after the meeting both are asked whether it happened. That way every successful appointment is recorded. Everything vertical-specific (labels, categories, credentials, listing fields) lives in a config row, so a second vertical is new data, not new code.

Appointments and how success is measured

A successful appointment is one where both sides say they met. One person's word isn't enough, so neither side can inflate the numbers.

StepWhat happensWhat it records
ProposeEither side picks a date, time and mode in the thread. The other accepts, suggests another time or declines.meeting_proposed
Meeting setOn accept, both numbers appear in the thread, and both get a Google Calendar invite (with a Google Meet link for online meetings). Before this, numbers and emails typed in chat are hidden.meeting_set, contact_unlocked_at, calendar_event_id
Check-inFor Google Meet meetings, Tulay reads who joined. If both did, it counts as met with no questions. Otherwise, and for in-person meetings, both get asked a few hours later: met, no-show, or rescheduled.Both "met" = success. Met vs no-show = disputed, goes to review. Rescheduled = propose a new time.
Follow-upTwo weeks later the client is asked if they got the help they needed. It doesn't ask whether they bought anything.satisfaction; unlocks a review

Main number to watch: appointments both sides confirmed, per week, per city. Supporting numbers: first-reply time, share of inquiries answered within 24 hours, proposal-to-meeting rate, no-show and dispute rate, outreach acceptance rate, and the follow-up "yes" rate. Advisors see their own numbers; the Tulay team sees the whole marketplace. Switch to Admin view at the top to try it.

Google Meet and calendars

  • One organizer account. A Tulay Google Workspace account owns every event. When a meeting is accepted, the server creates a Google Calendar event with both people as guests and asks Google to attach a Meet link (Calendar API events.insert with conferenceData.createRequest and conferenceDataVersion=1). Google emails the invite, so it lands in both people's calendars. Nobody has to connect their own Google account.
  • Changes follow. Rescheduling updates the same event and cancelling deletes it, with updates sent to guests, so calendars never show stale meetings.
  • In-app calendar. Each profile has a "My calendar" page built from Tulay's own appointments table, with "Join Google Meet" and "Open in Google Calendar" on each meeting. Past meetings stay listed as the record.
  • Attendance from Meet. After the meeting, the server reads the Meet REST API's conference record and participants to see who joined and when. Google deletes these records 30 days after the meeting, so save the join times right away. Only people signed in to Google show up by name, so anyone who joined anonymously falls back to the check-in.
  • Privacy. Set guestsCanSeeOtherGuests to false if guests shouldn't see each other's email. Don't record meetings by default; recording a financial conversation needs both people's consent.
  • Later: let people optionally connect their Google Calendar so Tulay can suggest times when both are free.

Rules that keep it fair for clients

  • Ranking never reads the subscription or the analytics. The feed only filters (category, radius, keyword) and sorts by distance or recency. There's no boosted or sponsored slot.
  • Numbers stay hidden until a meeting is set. Nobody's number leaves the app until both people have agreed on a time.
  • Clients are free, always. Only providers pay, and during the beta nobody does.
  • Reaching out is free, and each request has a limit. Advisors don't pay per reach-out. Up to 5 advisors can answer each client request, and a client who declines blocks that advisor. Paid plans never buy ranking or extra slots.
  • Outreach is opt-in. Advisors can only contact screened clients who turned on "open to contact". The advisor sees a first name, the city and the client's interests, never a number. The first message stays pending until the client accepts. Declining blocks that advisor.
  • Listings are moderated. No guaranteed returns, no recruitment ("be your own boss") posts, and a disclaimer on every financial listing. Reports and disputed check-ins go to an admin queue.

Suggested stack: Postgres with PostGIS for distance queries, a TypeScript web app (Next.js), and a hosted realtime service for messaging. Supabase covers Postgres, auth, realtime and storage in one, which suits a small team. Google Calendar and Meet REST APIs through one Workspace service account. A scheduled job reads Meet attendance and sends check-ins a few hours after each meeting's start time.

Compliance to settle before launch

  • Data Privacy Act (RA 10173): a privacy notice, a consent record for "open to contact" and for sharing numbers and emails (in the calendar invite) when a meeting is set, data-retention rules, and registration with the National Privacy Commission (NPC) if you meet its thresholds.
  • Check-in answers and follow-up feedback are personal data: say so in the privacy notice and keep them only as long as the metrics need.
  • Credential checks per vertical: Insurance Commission or SEC for advisors, PRC for dentists, NBI clearance for kasambahay. Store the evidence and the date checked.
  • Terms that say the platform connects people and isn't a party to the advice or contract.

Noted for later

  • One person in both roles: the membership table already allows it; the UI needs a role switcher like this prototype's.
  • Reviews: only after both sides confirmed they met, to limit fake reviews.
  • Verification at scale: manual checks are fine for the first hundreds; look at document upload plus spot checks after that.
  • Advisor feed posts that aren't listings (tips, updates): same table with a kind column, once moderation is in place.

Open questions

  • What does client screening check: identity, intent, both? It decides what sign-up asks for.
  • Which city do you launch in? A feed ranked by distance needs density in one place first.
  • Does an advisor's company (insurer or fund house) appear on listings? It helps trust but brings brand-use rules.
  • What happens after repeated no-shows? For example, a warning after two and a pause on messaging after three.
  • How should disputed check-ins be settled: ask both for details, or just track the pattern per user?
